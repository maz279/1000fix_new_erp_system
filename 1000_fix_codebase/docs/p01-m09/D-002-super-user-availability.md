## The arithmetic (with the assumption that carries the risk)

`GV-004` §6.3 / `CON-12` / `RSK-001` model UAT super-user demand at **2–3
super-users per module × 67 modules, at 30% of their time during UAT windows**:

| Reading | People | FTE-equivalent during UAT |
|---|---:|---:|
| Minimum (2/module) | **134** | ~40 |
| Maximum (3/module) | **201** | ~60 |

**The binding constraint on the whole programme is UAT super-user availability,
not developer capacity.** No organisation chart in the estate evidences 134–201
trained business staff — the model presumes branch managers, service leads and
back-office specialists exist in UAT-ready numbers, per module, per wave.

## Options put to the Board

| Option | Shape | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Confirm the model** | Named individuals per module, calendar-committed at 30% | Requires the roster to exist before Wave 1 ends; recruitment/training cost lands in W1–W2 |
| **B — Reduce UAT breadth** | 1 super-user per module + role-based pooled UAT for low-risk modules | Cuts demand to ~67; acceptance risk shifts to the pooled sessions — record the risk, don't hide it |
| **C — Phase the commitment** | Full 2–3 only for P0 modules (the fit-gap register's P0 set); others at 1 | Middle path; the P0 worklist from the P01-M03 register makes "P0 modules" a measured set |

## Recommendation

**Option C**, reviewed at each gate against actual UAT throughput — it converts
an unevidenced 134–201 promise into a measured, phased commitment, and it uses
the baseline the programme now owns (P01-M03's per-requirement priority column)
rather than a new estimate. Named individuals attach when the Board takes the
decision; `B-12` closes on that minute.
