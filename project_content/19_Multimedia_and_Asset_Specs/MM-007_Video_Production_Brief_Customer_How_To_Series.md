## 1. Purpose

`HC-020` is complete on **content**: 22 scripts, each derived from a published help article
(`VS-01`), each one task (`VS-05`), each under 90 seconds (`VS-03`), the library *"small by
design"* (`VS-16`). Its own scope note says where the rest belongs: **"This file holds scripts.
The produced video assets are `MM-007` … Scripts here; pixels there."** Its `O-01` states the
consequence plainly — *"Scripts can be written; **nothing can be filmed**."*

**What a production supplier still does not know, and cannot infer:**

| Absent | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Aspect ratio and framing per audience | A landscape video for a rider holding a phone in one hand at a customer's door |
| Whether Bengali is filmed or subtitled | `VS-07` says filmed; **a supplier who has not priced two shoots will deliver one and subtitle it** |
| The commitment boundary in presenter speech | A presenter saying "we'll be there within two hours" — a service-level promise that lives in `LG-015`, not in a video |
| Where screen content comes from | A screen recording taken from production, showing another customer's record |
| Encoding, captions and transcript as line items | 44 files with no captions and an `NFR-073` AA failure across the help centre |
| A withdrawal rule | Videos left up after a redesign, teaching a flow that no longer exists |

**Consequence of absence (`CP-03`).** The single highest-leverage item in the library —
`HC-020` script 9, *"Photograph your documents for registration"*, aimed at **the
highest-abandonment step in seller registration** against a **60% onboarding-completion target**
— is either not made, or made badly enough to be worse than the article it replaces.

**What this file is not.** It is not the scripts (`HC-020`), not the internal training series
(`MM-006` — different audience, different length, different gate), not the onboarding animations
(`MM-008`), and not the in-product placement (`HC-016`).

## 4. Content

### 4.1 The library, and what it costs to make

**Enumerated from `HC-020`, not estimated** (`CP-11`).

| | Count |
|---|---:|
| Scripts in the library | **22** |
| At `P1` | **8** |
| At `P2` | 11 |
| At `P3` | 3 |
| **Productions (× 2 languages, `VS-07`)** | **44** |
| Caption files (2 per production) | 88 |
| Transcripts | 44 |
| **Distinct audiences** | 5 — customer, seller, rider, technician, vendor |

**Composition by audience** (from `HC-020`'s library table):

| Audience | Scripts | Predominant form |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer (`P01`) | 8 | Screen recording with narration |
| Seller (`P07`) | 4 | **Live action + screen** — script 9 is physical |
| Rider (`P09`) | 5 | **Live action** — custody, packing, payment, no-signal |
| Technician (`P02`) | 4 | **Live action + screen** — evidence photography, sync check |
| Vendor (`P05`) | 1 | Screen recording |

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **CV-01** | **`HC-020` `VS-17`'s four are produced first**, in this order: **9** photograph your documents · **13** scan a device into your custody · **14** seal and pack · **18** take the photographs a claim needs. They are physical, spatial tasks performed by low-literacy users — precisely where a written article performs worst and where the format earns its cost |
| **CV-02** | **Then the eight `P1` items**, then `P2`, then `P3`. A partially produced library is normal and expected; **the order is what makes a partial library useful** |
| **CV-03** | **The library does not grow on the set.** A supplier who sees an obvious twenty-third video raises it to Content Design, who takes it through `VS-02` and `VS-16` — the library is small by design and each addition needs the case made |
| **CV-04** | **Bengali is produced first and English second**, on every item. Where budget stops the run, **the Bengali half is complete and the English half is not** — the same deliberate inversion `MM-006` `VT-14` makes, for the same audience reason |

### 4.2 Format and framing per audience

| Audience | Aspect | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer (`P01`)** | **9:16 vertical**, 1080 × 1920 master | Watched on a phone, in the app, one-handed |
| **Seller (`P07`)** | **9:16 vertical** | Same device, same posture; `M12`'s design position is *"independent engineers, not e-commerce merchants"* on a mid-range Android |
| **Rider (`P09`)** | **9:16 vertical** | Standing, one hand, often outdoors, often in a hurry |
| **Technician (`P02`)** | **9:16 vertical** for field tasks; **16:9** where two hands and a bench are in frame | The task decides, not the platform |
| **Vendor (`P05`)** | **16:9 landscape** | Watched at a desk |
| **Contact-centre use (`P10`)** | Whatever the audience's own format is | An agent sends the customer's version, not a separate one |

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **CV-05** | **Vertical is the default and landscape is the exception with a stated reason.** This inverts the usual production default; the reason is that four of five audiences watch on a phone held upright |
| **CV-06** | **A vertical video is framed vertically, not cropped from landscape.** A 16:9 shot cropped to 9:16 loses the hands, which on scripts 9, 13, 14 and 18 is the entire content |
| **CV-07** | **The subject is hands and objects, not a face.** These are demonstrations; a presenter's face at scale competes with the thing being shown |
| **CV-08** | **The video is comprehensible with the sound off.** Much of this audience watches in public, on a shared phone, or without headphones — the same reasoning `HC-020` `VS-08` gives for mandatory captions |
| **CV-09** | **First 5 seconds state what the video will let you do** (`VS-04`). No logo animation, no title card, no "welcome to 1000FiX" |
| **CV-10** | **90 seconds maximum** (`VS-03`). Over 90 seconds is two videos or the wrong format |

### 4.3 Live action and screen recording

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **CV-11** | **Screen recordings come from the sandbox and follow [`MM-002`](MM-002_Screenshot_Annotation_and_Redaction_Standard.md) §4.1–§4.4 in full** (`VS-09`, `HS-19`). **No customer-identifiable content, ever** — and frame-by-frame redaction is not a remedy, so it is discharged by not capturing |
| **CV-12** | Screen recordings are captured at the surface's own device class (`MM-002` §4.2): **360 × 800 logical for `P02`/`P09`, 390 × 844 for `P01`** — captured at 2× |
| **CV-13** | **No device frame, no hand-holding-phone mock-up, no zoom-and-pan, no click sounds, no mouse trails** (`MM-006` `VT-35`). Each makes a short demonstration harder to follow |
| **CV-14** | **Live-action documents on camera are sample documents** from [`TS-002`](../13_Test_and_Acceptance_Content/TS-002_Test_Data_Pack_and_Persona_Set.md). Script 9 shows a trade licence and an NID being photographed; **those are printed sample artefacts, never a real person's documents** — and this is the single highest-risk shot in the library |
| **CV-15** | **Devices on camera carry no recognisable manufacturer mark** (`MM-005` `IL-07`), for the same 17+-authorisation reason |
| **CV-16** | **Everyone on camera has recorded consent** (`MM-009` §4.2), and consent withdrawal triggers withdrawal or re-edit (`MM-006` `VT-46`) |
| **CV-17** | **No real customer, no real customer premises, no live job** (`MM-006` `VT-30`). Rider and technician scripts are filmed on a staged doorstep and a staged bench |
| **CV-18** | Where a script shows a **physical task with a safety dimension** — packing a lithium battery, handling a device with a swollen cell — **HSE approves the shot list and the cut** (`MM-006` `VT-25`, `KB-013`) |

### 4.4 Presenter, wardrobe and location

| Element | Brief |
|---|---|
| **Presenter** | A **Bengali-speaking practitioner**, not a professional presenter reading a script (`MM-006` `VT-13`). For rider and technician scripts, someone who does the job |
| **Voice** | The `VB-002` register for the audience — plain, direct, unhurried. **No enthusiasm, no sales tone** |
| **Wardrobe** | Ordinary Bangladeshi working dress; the 1000FiX uniform where one exists (`MM-005` `O-05` records that it is unspecified) |
| **Location** | A staged branch counter, workshop bench or doorstep. **Real locations only with `MM-009` consent and no third party in frame** |
| **Set dressing** | The real objects of this business (`MM-005` §4.2). No Western office props |
| **Lower-third** | **None** (`MM-006` `VT-23`) |
| **Brand presence** | A single mark, at the end frame only. **Not a watermark through the video** |

### 4.5 The commitment boundary — what a presenter may not say

**This is the section Legal reviews**, and it is why this file has a Legal reviewer at all.

`HC-020` `VS-11` states the rule: **no presenter claims, promises, prices or timings** — *"the
same commitment boundary as `HC-019`."* Applied to speech:

| A presenter may say | A presenter may **never** say |
|---|---|
| "You can track your repair here" | "Your repair will be ready in 48 hours" |
| "The price you see is the price you approve" | "Repairs start from BDT 500" |
| "You will get a message when it is ready" | "We will call you within an hour" |
| "Take a clear photograph of both sides" | "This guarantees your claim is approved" |
| "If it is not right, you can raise a complaint here" | "We will always refund you" |
| "Sellers set their own prices" | "You can earn BDT X a month" |
| "A rider will confirm the handover with you" | "Delivery is free" |

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **CV-19** | **A video never states a price, a rate, a fee or a commission.** Those live in `MD-029` and are communicated under [`EO-011`](../18_Ecosystem_Onboarding_Content/EO-011_Rate_Card_and_Pricing_Communication_Pack.md); a video cannot be re-cut when a rate card changes |
| **CV-20** | **A video never states a service level or a turnaround time.** Service levels live in [`LG-015`](../10_Legal_Policy_and_Compliance/LG-015_AMC_SLA_Master_Terms_and_Service_Credit_Rules.md) and `MD-028`; `HC-002` `HS-13` already separates a help *expectation* from an SLA *promise*, and a spoken promise is harder to qualify than a written one |
| **CV-21** | **A video never promises an outcome** — approval, refund, repair success, earnings. `EO-001` `SO-09` prohibits promising a seller a job volume or an income for the same reason |
| **CV-22** | **A video never contradicts `LG-002`, `LG-013` or `LG-014`.** Legal reviews every script's spoken content **before the shoot**, not the finished cut — a wrong sentence found in the cut is a reshoot |
| **CV-23** | **Where a script must reference terms, it points rather than paraphrases**: *"the full terms are in your account"* |

### 4.6 Language, captions and accessibility

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **CV-24** | **Bengali is authored and filmed, not subtitled** (`VS-07`). `HC-020`'s own troubleshooting table names the failure: *"Bengali videos unwatched → subtitled rather than filmed in Bengali → `VS-07` breached — reshoot"* |
| **CV-25** | **Captions in both languages are mandatory** (`VS-08`, WCAG SC 1.2.2), **WebVTT, authored, never burned in and never machine-generated** (`MM-001` `AC-01`, `AC-02`) |
| **CV-26** | A **transcript** ships with every video and is indexed by help search (SC 1.2.3, `MM-006` `VT-41`) |
| **CV-27** | **Audio description is avoided by writing narration that describes what is shown** (`MM-001` `AC-05`, `MM-006` `VT-40`) — a storyboard decision, not a post-production one |
| **CV-28** | **No auto-play with sound; no flashing** (SC 2.3.1); a poster frame from the first 5 seconds |
| **CV-29** | On-screen text meets the `VB-009` §3 Bengali floors **at the L1 encoding rung** (`MM-001` `VE-10`) and never duplicates the narration |
| **CV-30** | **UI labels spoken and shown match `UI-002`/`UI-003`** (`VS-10`), in the language of the interface being shown |

### 4.7 Deliverable specification

**The `CG-009` §3 required addition for Category 19.**

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Master format** | **ProRes 422 or DNxHD**; **1080 × 1920 vertical** (default) or 1920 × 1080 landscape (stated exception); 25 fps |
| **Delivery formats** | **H.264 High Profile ≤ L4.0** at the `MM-001` §4.5 ladder, **vertical rungs**: 240 × 426 / 360 × 640 / 480 × 854 / 720 × 1280; plus progressive MP4 at L2 |
| **Ceiling** | **720p equivalent** (`MM-001` `VE-02`) |
| **Duration** | **≤ 90 seconds** (`VS-03`); first 5 s state the outcome |
| **Audio** | AAC-LC 128 kbps; **−16 LUFS**, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; **comprehensible with sound off** (`CV-08`) |
| **Captions** | **WebVTT, `bn` and `en`, separate, authored** |
| **Transcript** | Structured document per language, indexed by help search |
| **Poster** | Real frame from the first 5 s |
| **Per-surface encodes** | Help centre (HLS + MP4) · in-product `HC-016` (MP4 L2, muted, captions on by default) · contact centre (MP4 L2, shareable link) · **WhatsApp (MP4 ≤ 16 MB, vertical)** |
| **Metadata** | Title, language, duration, **`HC-020` script number**, **source help article** (`VS-01`), audience, release captured against |
| **Naming** | `MM-012` §4.3, carrying the `HC-020` script number |
| **Source** | Project file, uncut footage, audio stems, graphics source (`MM-001` `DL-02`) |
| **Evidence** | **Legal review of spoken content before the shoot** (`CV-22`) · HSE approval where §4.3 `CV-18` applies · consent records (`CV-16`) · storyboard approval (`VS-13`) |

### 4.8 Distribution and the contact centre

| Surface | Encode | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Help centre article** | HLS ladder + MP4 L2 | Embedded beside the source article, never instead of it (`VS-01`) |
| **In-product** ([`HC-016`](../09_Help_Centre_and_Self_Service/HC-016_In_Product_Help_Tour_and_Coach_Mark_Copy.md)) | MP4 L2 | **Muted by default, captions on, dismissible** (`PT-22`, `PT-24` — no timed auto-advance) |
| **Contact centre** (`P10`) | MP4 L2 shareable link | An agent sends it **during** a call, and the link does not expire mid-conversation |
| **WhatsApp** ([`NT-008`](../04_Notification_and_Messaging/04B_WhatsApp_and_Push/NT-008_WhatsApp_Business_Template_Library.md)) | MP4 ≤ 16 MB vertical | **Subject to the template and consent rules in `NT-008` and `NT-002`** — a video is a message, not an exemption |
| **Public / marketing** | **Not this library** | Marketing video is the website programme's, under `CB-001` |

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **CV-31** | **The video never replaces the article** (`VS-01`). `HC-020`'s own troubleshooting row — *"watched but task still failed → the video shows the happy path only"* — is why the article, which carries the failure cases, remains |
| **CV-32** | **A video is not sent unsolicited.** Distribution through WhatsApp or SMS obeys [`NT-002`](../04_Notification_and_Messaging/NT-002_Notification_Preference_and_Consent_Copy.md) consent and [`NT-003`](../04_Notification_and_Messaging/NT-003_Quiet_Hours_Throttling_and_Frequency_Policy.md) frequency rules, and **the data cost of a video sent to a customer is the customer's** |
| **CV-33** | **In-product video does not paper over a confusing interface** (`HC-016` `PT-11`, `PT-28`). Where a screen needs a video to be usable, that is product feedback and it is recorded as such |

### 4.9 Reshoot economics and withdrawal

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| **CV-34** | **`VS-15` governs: a video orphaned by a release is withdrawn, not left.** An article is edited in minutes; a video needs a crew, a presenter, a translation and a production cycle |
| **CV-35** | **A reshoot reserve is budgeted at commissioning.** `HC-020` `O-06` states it exactly: *"a video library without a reshoot budget decays faster than an article set."* A library with no reserve will be withdrawn item by item until it is empty |
| **CV-36** | **Screen-recorded items (the customer and vendor sets) decay fastest** and are commissioned **after** their portal's UI has stabilised — the same sequencing `MM-006` `VT-05` applies to S4 |
| **CV-37** | **Live-action physical items (`VS-17`'s four) decay slowest.** Photographing a document and sealing a package do not change with a release. **This is a second reason they are produced first** |
| **CV-38** | Withdrawal removes the item from **every** surface — help, in-product, contact-centre link, WhatsApp template library and any cache — before the correction publishes (`MM-002` `SC-30` pattern) |
