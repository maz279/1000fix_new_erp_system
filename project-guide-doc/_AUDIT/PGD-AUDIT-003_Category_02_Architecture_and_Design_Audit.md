## 1. Purpose and Method

This audit is **mandatory under the commissioning instruction** and was performed on the thirteen
Category 02 documents immediately after authoring, before any consumer relied on them.

It applies the four methods `PGD-AUDIT-001` established, plus one added because this category makes
**measured claims about 67 specification files** rather than claims about a register:

| # | Method | What was checked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Structural consistency** | Front-matter completeness, house-template section conformance, identifier uniqueness, link integrity |
| 2 | **Arithmetic consistency** | Every roll-up figure regenerated from its source rows immediately before issue (`PG-M10`) |
| 3 | **External verification** | Every extraction re-executed against the filesystem; commands recorded so the analysis is reproducible |
| 4 | **Temporal verification** | Whether the estate changed during authoring |
| 5 | **Cross-document consistency** *(added)* | Whether a figure measured in one document is used consistently in the other twelve |

**Result: 18 findings — 3 critical, 7 high, 6 medium, 2 low. Seven are closed; eleven remain open.**

> **Three findings are against this authoring session's own work** — `PGD-A3-16`, `PGD-A3-17` and
> `PGD-A3-18`. The first two are the same defect class the estate's three prior audits have each
> found: **a figure quoted rather than counted, and a summary written before the rows it summarises
> were finished.** The third is an **identifier collision with a concurrent authoring session**. All
> three are closed. They are recorded rather than silently corrected, because a document set that
> names `PG-03` in every preamble and then breaches it cannot ask the rest of the programme to
> disclose its own errors.
>
> ### ⚠ Concurrent authoring — this audit was renumbered before issue
>
> **Category 00 and Category 01 were authored by other sessions while this audit was being written.**
> Enumerating `project-guide-doc/` at **03:10** returned **58 files**, not the 22 the register
> records: 11 `GV` documents, 10 `RQ` documents, these 13 `AR` documents, and **two audits both
> claiming the identifier `PGD-AUDIT-002`.**
>
> This audit was issued as `PGD-AUDIT-002` at 03:03 and **renumbered to `PGD-AUDIT-003` before
> release**, with its finding prefix moved from `A2-nn` to `PGD-A3-nn`. See `PGD-A3-18`.

# 3. Critical Findings

## `PGD-A3-04` — The unregistered-event gap is 953, not 21

**Severity: Critical · Type: Measured omission · Status: OPEN**

**What was found.** Extraction of the `### 12.3 Domain Events Published` table from all 67 module
specification files returns **988 distinct declared events**. `05-03` §4.2 registers **64**.

| Measure | Count |
|---|---:|
| Declared published (module §12.3) | **988** |
| Registered (`05-03` §4.2) | **64** |
| Declared but unregistered | **953** |
| Registered but published by no module | **29** |
| Consumed with no producer anywhere | **8** |
| Distinct namespaces | **243**, of which **~40 use a module-code convention covering ~245 events** |

**Relationship to `AP-01`.** Audit `AP-01` (critical, `AUDIT-016`) compared three files — `05-03`
§3.3, `P04` §12.3 and `P08` §12.3 — and found 21 unregistered names, correctly classifying 14 as
genuinely missing. **That finding is sound; its scale was set by its sample.** The full extraction
across all 67 files is approximately **45× larger**.

**Why it is Critical.** `AP-01` posed the question directly and could not answer it: *"If the
inventory is deliberately partial, then `FR-IPF-032`'s living catalogue will be the first document
that reveals the true event count, at which point every consumer specification written against the
inventory is incomplete."* **That is now measured. `05-03` §4.2 covers 6.5% of the event surface.**

Three of the missing events are controls, not conveniences:

| Event | What depends on it |
|---|---|
| `warranty_claim.evidence_incomplete` | **Suppresses claim submission** on an incomplete evidence pack. The W4 gate's 90% first-submission approval target rests on it |
| `recall.execution_updated` | **Product safety** — `P08` `FR-POR-254` |
| `credit_note.issued` | **The SLA remedy** — `P04` `AC-10` requires service credits published without prompting |

**Also found: two incompatible naming conventions in active use.** `service_order.completed` — the
worked example in `02-01` §7, cited in `05-03` §4.2, in `AP-003` content and in multiple portal
specifications — **is declared published by no module's §12.3.**

**Remediation.** `AR-009` §10.6 RM-01…RM-09, with owners and waves. **RM-01 (ratify ADR-017) and
RM-03 (~245 renames) are Wave 1 and are cheapest now, because no consumer code exists.** Once
consumers exist, `05-03` §4.3 forbids breaking changes within a `schema_version` and the rename
becomes a dual-publish migration across 67 modules and 12 surfaces.

**Cannot be closed here.** It closes with `IN-003` publishing 988 registrations.

## `PGD-A3-09` — Thirty-two wave-order violations

**Severity: Critical · Type: Sequencing defect · Status: OPEN**

**What was found.** `_MODULE_TEMPLATE` §16.3 requires *"No prerequisite may land in a later wave than
this module."* Computing that rule over the declared dependency graph — 67 nodes, **447 edges** —
returns **32 violations**.

**Why it went undetected.** The rule is applied per file. An author checking M02's prerequisites
cannot see whether M22's wave changed after M02 was written. **`02-04_Module_Dependency_Map.md` was
planned and never written**, so nobody ran the join.

**The most serious single violation:**

> **M29 SEC (Wave 1) declares a dependency on M21 HCM (Wave 5) — a gap of four waves.**
> **M29 has an in-degree of 53**; 79% of the estate depends on it. Its access lifecycle is driven by
> `employee.joined` / `employee.left` from M21.
>
> Without correction, Waves 1–4 have **manual access provisioning**, so `KPI-090` (*zero orphaned
> accounts beyond 24 hours*) is **unachievable for four waves** in a system holding payment
> credentials and personal data — and retrofitting event-driven provisioning into a live Keycloak
> realm in Wave 5 is precisely the risk ADR-023 exists to avoid.

**A structural pattern, not a list of accidents.** Four of the eight most-depended-on modules sit
later in the plan than modules depending on them:

| Module | In-degree | Wave | Dependants in earlier waves |
|---|---:|:--:|---|
| **M24** DMS — owns the `fix.document` **kernel** | 23 | **W3** | 17 modules in W1–W2 attach documents |
| **M17** AST | 17 | W2 | M01, M04 (both W1) |
| **M21** HCM | 15 | W5 | M29 (W1), M52 (W3), + 8 |
| M33 CPM — party kernel | 41 | W1 | — (correctly placed) |

**High in-degree plus a late wave is the signature of a wave plan built from business priority rather
than from the dependency graph.** That is not a criticism of the plan's intent; it is the arithmetic
it was never checked against.

**Remediation.** `AR-006` §5 dispositions all 32 — 14 STUB, 9 MOVE, 7 NOT-A-PREREQ, 2 ACCEPT — and
§5.5 requires **five scope movements**:

| # | Movement | From | To |
|---|---|:--:|:--:|
| MV-01 | M21 employee master + joiner/mover/leaver events | W5 | **W1** |
| MV-02 | M24 `fix.document` kernel + retention + attachment API | W3 | **W1** |
| MV-03 | M51 VAT determination + Mushak 6.3 issuance | W4 | **W3** |
| MV-04 | M64 MDM governance core | W2 | **W1** |
| MV-05 | M17 device registry core — declaration correction only | W2 | W1 |

**These resolve 19 of the 32 directly and 6 more by consequence.** They add scope to Wave 1 — the
wave `AR-001` §11.3 already identifies as understated by module count.

**Cannot be closed here.** Requires a Steering decision on Wave 1 scope (`GV-004`, `AR-006` Q-01).
**If MV-01 is refused, four waves of manual access provisioning must be accepted explicitly as a
risk, not arrive by omission** (`AR-006` Q-04).

## `PGD-A3-13` — The entity catalogue is stale by approximately six times

**Severity: Critical · Type: Stale baseline with a downstream sizing consequence · Status: OPEN**

**What was found.** `05-02_Data_Model_and_Entity_Catalogue.md` is titled *"225 Entities Across 30
Modules"*. Extracting the `### 10.1 Owned Entities` table from all 67 specifications returns:

| Measure | Count |
|---|---:|
| Owned-entity rows declared | **1,369** |
| **Distinct entities declared owned** | **1,334** |
| Catalogued in `05-02` | **225** |
| **Coverage** | **~17%** |

**Why it is Critical.** `05-02` is correct for what it covers and was simply never restated for the
67-module architecture. The consequence is not documentary: **`DA-001` (Database Schema
Specification) is P0, Wave 1, and sizing it from 225 entities under-scopes it by roughly 83%.**
`DA-001` is on the critical path for all 58 custom Odoo modules.

**Also found: 34 entities are declared as owned by two or three modules** — including `fix.zone`
(M02 **and** M30, a shared-kernel concept with two owners), `fix.asset` (M17 and M49), `fix.sod.rule`
(M29 and M60) and `fix.processing.record` (M33 and M60, a PDPA artefact). All 34 are adjudicated in
`AR-008` §7: **14 renames, 17 single-owner rulings, 3 splits.**

**A finding in the other direction, recorded for balance.** `_MODULE_TEMPLATE` §10.2 warns that
*"255 references across the estate currently name entities their declared owner does not define — the
single largest technical defect found in the August 2026 audit."* **Reconciling all 274
referenced-entity rows against their declared owner's §10.1 returns zero mismatches.** That defect
has been **fully remediated**. It is the clearest evidence in the estate that the audit culture
closes findings rather than only recording them.

**Cannot be closed here.** Requires a decision on whether `05-02` is reissued for 67 modules or
superseded by `DA-001` (`AR-008` Q-01).

**Severity: High · Type: Arithmetic inconsistency · Status: CLOSED at issue**

**What was found.** `AR-012` stated *"31 functions"* in **seven** places — §1.1, §2.2, §4, §10, AC-02,

|---|---:|

## `PGD-A3-11` — Fifty-five reciprocity failures

**Severity: Medium · Type: Declaration asymmetry · Status: OPEN**

Rule `RG-04` requires that if A declares a dependency on B, B declares A in its *"Depended on by"*
row. Excluding the four modules permitted the blanket phrase (M28, M29, M30, M65), **55 declarations
are one-way**:

| Target | Failures |
|---|---:|
| **M33** CPM (party kernel) | **22** |
| **M21** HCM | **11** |
| **M24** DMS (document kernel) | **8** |
| M19 FIN | 4 |
| M27 CCE | 3 |
| 10 others | 1–2 each |

**Why it matters more than it looks.** A one-way declaration means **the owning module does not know
it is depended on**. M33 can change the party-role model and 22 modules find out at integration;
slipping M21 looks like it affects 4 modules when it affects 15.

**Remediation.** `AR-006` §7.3 — kernel owners adopt the blanket phrase where genuinely universal;
the remainder complete their lists; and **`AR-012` `FF-14` makes reciprocity a CI check**, because 55
failures accumulated precisely because nothing checked.
