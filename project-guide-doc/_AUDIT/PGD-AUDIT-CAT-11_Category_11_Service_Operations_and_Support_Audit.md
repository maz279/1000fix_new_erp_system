**What was found.** `SV-003` §5.3 mapped the five issued component runbooks (`OP-007`…`OP-011`)

### `SVA-02` — The platform on-call rota is one person short of its own sustainability rule

**Severity: HIGH · Status: OPEN · Owner: CTO with HR Director**

**What was found.** `OP-013` §4.3 states that a 24×7 primary rota on weekly shifts needs **six**
people, and separately requires a **secondary** rota staffed *"6 (same pool, offset)"*. `OP-013`
`RT-04` caps on-call at **one week in three sustained**. Following the arithmetic through:

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| On-call person-weeks per year | 2 positions × 52 weeks | **104** |
| Pool of 6, ignoring leave | 104 ÷ 6 = 17.3 wk each | **1 in 3.0 — exactly at the `RT-04` ceiling** |
| Available weeks per person (≈ 5 weeks leave and public holidays) | 52 − 5 | 47 |
| Pool of 6, with leave | 104 ÷ (6 × 47) | **1 in 2.7 — `RT-04` breached** |
| Pool required | 104 ÷ (47 ÷ 3) = 6.64 | **7** |
| Pool of 7, checked | 104 ÷ (7 × 47) | **1 in 3.16 ✓** |

**`OP-013` states the primary arithmetic correctly and completely and does not carry it to the
second rota.** Recomputed independently by this audit; the figures reproduce.

**Consequence.** `AUDIT-017` `OP-09`'s *"the rota needs ten people and has none"* understates it:
the requirement is **eleven**, and at six the rota is in breach of its own rule the first time
anyone takes leave.

**Action.** Steering decision on `SV-004` `SV-004-D-03`. And note `SV-005` `SV-005-D-02`: if
hypercare's ring-fenced engineers are drawn from the same pool, **the pool is eight, not seven**.

### `SVA-05` — Hypercare is ≈ 45% of the build estimate and appears in neither published estimate

**Severity: HIGH · Status: OPEN · Owner: CFO with Programme Manager**

**What was found.** `OP-002` §4.3 sets a hypercare duration per wave. Summed: **56 weeks across a
156-week programme — 36% of the delivery period** — planned as a single event. Applying `OP-002`
§4.1's own staffing rule to those 56 weeks gives three cost pools:

| Pool | Effort | Owner | Competes with |
|---|--:|---|---|
| Project — ring-fenced engineers + coordination | **288 PW** | Programme / CTO | The **1,513 PW** build estimate |
| Support — T1 at 2× baseline | **250 PW** | Head of ITSM / CFO | An establishment that is itself unfunded |
| **Business — floor-walking and ride-alongs** | **≈ 140 PW** | Operations Director | **Nothing. It is in no estimate** |
| **Total** | **≈ 678 PW** | | **≈ 45% of the build estimate** |

Recomputed by this audit: 288 + 250 + 140 = 678; 678 ÷ 1,513 = 44.8%; 678 PW ÷ 156 weeks = 4.3 FTE
sustained for three years. **The figures reproduce**, and 4.3 FTE to stabilise nine waves across
51+ branches is conservative rather than inflated.

**The third pool is the finding.** It is paid in **branch staff time** — not build, not content, not
any branch budget. It is also the pool cut first, and `OP-002` §4.4 says so: floor-walking *"is the
single highest-yield hypercare activity and the first one cut when resourcing is short."*

**A fifth item for `AUDIT-017` §4's unevidenceable list: the programme cannot evidence that it has
resourced its own stabilisation.** Unlike the other four, no infrastructure purchase closes it.

**Action.** `HCT-Q-01` and `HCT-Q-02`, both before W1 cutover.
