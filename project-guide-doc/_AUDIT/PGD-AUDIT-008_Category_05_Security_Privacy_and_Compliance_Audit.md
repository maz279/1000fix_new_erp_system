# 3. Critical Findings

## `SCA-11` — The audit trail defeats residency unless it splits across `Z3` and `Z4`

**Severity: Critical · Type: Design defect with statutory consequence · Status: OPEN**

**What was found.** `NFR-014` and `M29` §10.1 require the audit entry `fix.audit.entry` to record
`old_value` and `new_value` for every change to a controlled object. `M29` places that entity in the
regional store.

`NFR-086` requires residency-classified fields — NID, passport, TIN, biometric templates, recovered
customer data, seller KYC, criminal records — to be held in a **Bangladesh-resident** store and
**never duplicated into the regional store, not transiently, not in a cache, not in a log**.

**These two requirements are incompatible as specified.** A change to a customer's NID writes an
audit entry containing the NID's before-value and after-value. If that entry lands in `Z3`, **the
audit trail becomes a regional copy of a resident value** — and it does so through the one mechanism
in the estate deliberately designed to be complete, inherited, and impossible for a module to opt
out of (`SEC-SD-4`, `M29` `T-02`).

**Why it is critical.** Three reasons compound:

| # | |
|---|---|
| 1 | **It is silent.** Nothing fails. The audit works, residency enforcement works on the *business* record, and the leak is in the trail nobody reads until an investigation |
| 2 | **It is complete.** Audit is inherited and cannot be opted out of, so **every** residency-classified field change leaks, not some |
| 3 | **It is retained for five years** (`NFR-095`), so the exposure accumulates rather than ages out |

**Position taken.** `SC-010` §6.3: the trail splits the way the data splits. A change to a
residency-classified field writes its `L1` entry to a **resident audit partition in `Z4`**, joined
to the regional trail by the correlation id. The regional entry records **that** the field changed,
by whom and when; the resident entry records **what it changed from and to**.

**Action.** **This changes the `M29` §10.1 `fix.audit.entry` design and must be resolved before W1
build**, because retrofitting an audit-store split across live data is exactly the class of change
`AR-001` `TR-13` warns cannot be done cheaply after go-live. Owner: Chief Architect with the CDO.
Raised to `M29`, `AR-001` and `SC-007`. `SC-010` `Q-01`.

## `SCA-20` — Nothing is under version control, and every build-security gate depends on it

**Severity: Critical · Type: Precondition absent · Status: OPEN**

**What was found.** `PGD-A2-15` records **644+ files, zero under version control**. Re-verified at
20:15 (+06): still zero.

NIST SP 800-218 SSDF practice **`PS.1` — protect all forms of code from unauthorised access and
tampering** — is the practice every other build control rests on. **With no version control there is
no baseline, no attributable change history, and no way to detect tampering.** Signed artefacts
(`G-12`), build provenance, protected branches and four-eyes review are all meaningless without it.

`SC-005` therefore raises it as **`G-00` — a precondition, not a gate**. `SC-002` `TH-075` rates
CI/CD compromise **Critical** and `TH-094` rates supply-chain compromise **Critical**, and neither
has a mitigating control until `G-00` is met.

**Why this sits in Category 05 rather than Category 07.** It is a security precondition before it is
an engineering convenience: it is the control that makes `A08:2025` (software and data integrity
failures) and `A03:2025` (supply-chain failures) addressable at all.

**Action.** Establish version control for all source, configuration, IaC and identity profiles, with
protected branches, in **W1 week 1**. Owner: CTO with Platform Engineering. `SC-005` `Q-01`.

## `SCA-22` — There is no lawful disaster-recovery path for `Z4`, and option C was chosen by default

**Severity: Critical · Type: Regulatory exposure · Status: OPEN**

**What was found.** `AUDIT-017` `OP-01` established that `OP-012` §4.3's three recovery routes for
the Bangladesh-resident store include only one lawful full recovery, and **it is unavailable**.
`SC-007` §9 re-assessed all three at 14:25 (+06) and confirms the position unchanged:

| Route | State |
|---|---|
| 1. Operate without the sensitive fields | ✔ Available — **degraded, and only for an *unreachable* store, not a *lost* one** |
| 2. Restore from a resident backup to a **resident** target | ✖ **No resident DR target exists** |
| 3. Restore into the regional environment | ✖ **A regulatory act** under `NFR-086b` |

**What this audit adds to `OP-01`.** `SC-007` §9.4 costs three options and finds that **`Z4` is
sized by regulation, not by volume** (`AR-001` §8.6: *"Bangladesh-resident store — regulatory, not
volume — small, but availability-critical"*). A resident DR target is therefore **materially cheaper
than a full second environment**, which changes the decision.

> **The finding this audit makes beyond `OP-01`:** the programme is currently on **option C** —
> accepting that `NFR-007` does not apply to residency-classified data — **without having decided
> it.** `OP-01` says the constraint is *"invisible until the night it binds"*. A risk taken by
> omission is the one nobody owns, and it is the one that gets resolved at 02:00 by an engineer
> choosing between a two-hour RTO and a statutory breach.

**Action.** Decide, at W2: **A + B recommended** — provision a Bangladesh-resident DR target
(with in-country key escrow, `SC-006` `ES-3`) **and** obtain the Authority's written position for
the residual case. If neither, record option C as a **board-level risk acceptance** and amend
`NFR-007` and `OP-012` to say so. Owner: CTO with CFO and Legal. `GV-005` `RSK-SC-01`.

## `SCA-24` — Biometric attendance proceeds only on a condition not yet met

## `SCA-25` — `M06` certificates outlive their signature algorithm

**NIST IR 8547**: 112-bit-strength classical algorithms — RSA-2048, ECDSA P-256 — are **deprecated
after 2030 and disallowed after 2035**.

`M06` destruction certificates and `LG-019` e-signature evidence are relied on for the life of the
asset and any dispute arising from it — plausibly a decade or more. **A certificate signed in 2027
is inside its evidential life when its signature algorithm becomes disallowed.**

`LG-021` §4.4.6 identified the gap precisely and left it open: *"`LG-019` `O-07` records that no

## 9. Closure

**27 findings — 7 closed, 20 open.** The twenty open findings are **not defects in the eleven
documents**; with three exceptions (`SCA-04`, `SCA-27`, and the `SCA-11` design decision) they are
conditions in the estate and the organisation that the documents surfaced and cannot themselves
close.

**What Category 05 changed.** Before 2026-08-18 the source estate contained **zero threat models and
zero assessments of any kind**, and the four critical regulatory findings `C-04a`, `C-04b`, `C-05`
and `OP-01` had no engineering artefact behind them. It now has a threat model with 97 enumerated
threats, a DPIA that answers `HC-01` with a stop, a residency design that closes `OP-01`'s design
half with three costed options, and a currency register that implements the control `00-06` §9
proposed and nobody built.

**What it did not change.** There is still no CISO, no CDO and no Security Lead. Nothing is under
version control. Break-glass does not exist. Nobody is on call. **Four of those are organisational,
cheap, and closeable before a line of code is written** — and they are the four that will hurt most
at 02:00.

> **The honest summary.** This category's documents are good specifications of controls that do not
> exist, written by an authoring function auditing itself, for roles nobody holds. That is a real
> and necessary step forward from having neither the documents nor the controls — and it is not the
> same thing as being secure. `GV-009` wave gates are where the distinction stops being academic.
